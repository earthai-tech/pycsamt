# M17 — 3-D Unstructured (Tetrahedral) MT Solver Feasibility and Architecture Decision Record

Status: decided (gate evaluated 2026-08-05)
Scope: a proposed `mt3d_tri` backend in `pycsamt.forward.maxwell` — a Nédélec-edge-element MT solver on unstructured tetrahedral meshes, the 3-D unstructured counterpart of `mt2d_tri`/`trifem2d.py` (M11-M16) and the unstructured counterpart of `mt3d.py`/`modem3d.py` (M6/M7).
Rule: per the same discipline `mt3d.py` was gated by (`AI-INVERSION-M6-3D-ADR.md`), no in-house `mt3d_tri` module may be built until this record's gate is evaluated; a failed production gate does not by itself forbid a research-only prototype (see `mt3d.py`'s own precedent), but the ceiling that prototype may safely run at must come from measured numbers, not a guess.

## 1. Governing equation and chosen discretization

Same governing equation as M6/`mt3d.py`, `curl(mu^-1 curl E) + i*omega*sigma*E = source`, but discretized on an unstructured tetrahedral mesh with **first-order Nédélec (Whitney) edge elements**, not nodal (Lagrange) elements. This was a deliberate choice over the simpler nodal generalization of `tri_fem2d.py`'s 2-D approach: 2-D MT decouples into two independent *scalar* Helmholtz equations (TE/TM), for which nodal FEM is exactly right and is what M16 validated; full 3-D MT has no such decoupling, and nodal elements applied directly to the vector curl-curl equation are known to admit spurious non-physical modes at conductivity discontinuities, because they force continuity of every field component across element faces rather than only the tangential component. Edge elements enforce exactly the right (tangential-continuous, normal-discontinuous) continuity and are the standard choice in the published 3-D EM FEM literature (Jin, *The Finite Element Method in Electromagnetics*). This also keeps `mt3d_tri` in the same field-placement family as `mt3d.py`'s own Yee staggered grid (E on edges, H on faces) — DOF live on mesh edges in both, just structured vs. unstructured.

## 2. What was actually checked (not assumed)

- Repo-wide search for `tetgen|tetrahed|prism` in `pycsamt/**/*.py`: zero matches (confirmed in the exploration pass ahead of this ADR). No 3-D unstructured mesh generator exists anywhere in this codebase today.
- Direct probe of `tetgen`, `pygmsh`, `gmsh` in the environment used for `pycsamt` development (`import` attempted for each): **none installed**.
- `pip install --dry-run tetgen`: network-reachable, resolvable, but pulls in `pyvista`, `vtk` (~9.5, a large binary wheel), and `numpy 2.0.2` as transitive dependencies — a materially heavier and currently-unpinned dependency chain than `triangle` (M11's 2-D dependency, a thin Shewchuk-Triangle binding with no such transitive weight). This is flagged as an open item for a future M18, not resolved here.
- **No new dependency was installed to run this gate.** `scipy.spatial.Delaunay` (already a project dependency, Qhull-backed) can tetrahedralize a 3-D point cloud directly, with no boundary/PSLG constraints and no quality grading control — unsuitable for production meshing (no topography-following boundary, no station-forced vertices, no `triangle`-style `pq<angle>` quality flags), but sufficient to get a **real** tetrahedral mesh's vertex/tet/edge counts and edge-adjacency sparsity pattern for a feasibility benchmark, rather than a hand-guessed stencil. This is a stronger empirical basis than M6's own gate had (M6 used a synthetic 7-point-stencil proxy with no real mesh backing it at all, since no meshing tool was in scope there either).

## 3. Feasibility benchmark: real tetrahedral edge-DOF scaling, direct vs. iterative

**Method.** Six point clouds of increasing density (`n_core` = 200 to 8,000 target interior points, each with a geometrically-graded 6-ray padding shell mimicking a receiver-graded MT mesh) were tetrahedralized with `scipy.spatial.Delaunay`. For each mesh, the real edge set was extracted from the tetrahedra (the true first-order Nédélec DOF set), and a complex-valued sparse matrix was built on the **real edge-adjacency sparsity pattern** (two edges coupled iff they co-occur in a shared tet — exactly the fill pattern a real 6x6-per-tet Nédélec element assembly produces), with a diagonally-dominant diagonal (`4.0 + 0.5j`, real curl-curl-like + imaginary mass-like term) and small degree-normalized off-diagonal coupling. As in M6 §3, **this is a computational proxy for cost scaling, not a physical Maxwell solve** — the matrix is deliberately well-conditioned, unlike a real indefinite curl-curl system. Timed with `scipy 1.10.0`, `spsolve` (direct, SuperLU) and `bicgstab` (iterative, unpreconditioned, `tol=1e-8`), wall-clock via `time.perf_counter`:

| n_core | V (nodes) | T (tets) | E = DOF (edges) | E/V | nnz | assemble | direct `spsolve` | iterative `bicgstab` | iters |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 200 | 258 | 1,910 | 2,175 | 8.43 | 36,603 | 0.016 s | 0.158 s | 0.014 s | 4 |
| 500 | 554 | 3,970 | 4,530 | 8.18 | 76,032 | 0.021 s | 0.867 s | 0.002 s | 4 |
| 1,000 | 1,054 | 7,733 | 8,793 | 8.34 | 148,029 | 0.042 s | 4.295 s | 0.004 s | 4 |
| 2,000 | 2,269 | 16,674 | 18,951 | 8.35 | 319,137 | 0.110 s | 28.757 s | 0.007 s | 4 |
| 4,000 | 4,186 | 30,978 | 35,173 | 8.40 | 592,837 | 0.205 s | 105.374 s | 0.017 s | 4 |
| 8,000 | 8,114 | 59,689 | 67,811 | 8.36 | 1,142,267 | 0.405 s | **804.672 s** | 0.187 s | 4 |

Real, measured structural facts: `T/V ≈ 7.2-7.4` and `E/V ≈ 8.2-8.4` across all six meshes, consistent with the known asymptotic complexity of 3-D Delaunay tetrahedralizations of graded point sets (Dwyer-type results put random-point 3-D Delaunay near `T≈6.6V`, `E≈7.7V`; the somewhat higher ratios here are expected given the anisotropic graded padding shell). Assembly (mesh build + edge extraction + pattern construction) is cheap and scales roughly linearly with DOF (0.016 s → 0.405 s over a 31x DOF increase) — not the bottleneck.

Direct-solve time is the finding. Fitting the six measured points in log-log space gives an empirical exponent of **~2.48** (`ln(804.672/0.158) / ln(67811/2175) = 8.535 / 3.439 ≈ 2.48`) — markedly worse than M6's own structured-grid proxy exponent of ~2.1 (M6 §3), consistent with the well-known fact that unstructured, especially anisotropically-graded, 3-D meshes have materially worse nested-dissection separators than a plain Cartesian grid, hence more LU fill-in (Davis, *Direct Methods for Sparse Linear Systems*, SIAM 2006 — the same reference M6 cites). Concretely: **a direct solve at only 67,811 DOF — smaller than a single structured-grid production mesh, and far below M6's own 216,000-512,000-cell production target — already takes 13.4 minutes for one polarization/frequency.** M6's structured proxy did not cross into "clearly infeasible" territory until extrapolated out to a 60-80-cells-per-axis production mesh; this unstructured case is already unambiguously infeasible for dataset generation at a mesh size well inside what a real graded tet mesh would need to resolve station spacing and multi-skin-depth extent simultaneously.

The unpreconditioned `bicgstab` iterative solve stayed trivially fast (4 iterations, every size, `resid ≈ 1e-9`) — but per the exact caution M6 gave for its own proxy, **this cannot be read as evidence that iterative 3-D unstructured EM solves are easy**: the proxy's off-diagonal coupling was deliberately kept small relative to the diagonal to stay well-conditioned, and its 4-iteration convergence regardless of problem size is itself a sign the proxy is *too* easy, not a real indicator. A real complex-symmetric, indefinite curl-curl system is exactly the case the published 3-D EM literature (ModEM, SimPEG, emg3d — cited identically in M6 §3) universally pairs with a problem-specific preconditioner (multigrid or shifted-Laplacian), never bare Krylov iteration. **This project has no such preconditioner today, for either the structured (`mt3d.py`) or unstructured case.**

## 4. Decisions

### 4.1 Element type: confirmed, Nédélec edge elements

Per §1 — no change; recorded here because this ADR is also the record of that choice, made with the person requesting this work before the benchmark was run.

### 4.2 Mesh generator, if M18 is pursued later

**Not committed by this ADR.** `scipy.spatial.Delaunay` (used above) is unsuitable for production meshing (no PSLG/boundary constraints, no topography-following surface, no station-forced vertices, no quality grading control — the exact capabilities `build_graded_tri_mesh` provides for the 2-D case via `triangle`). If a mesh generator is added later, `tetgen` is the natural choice (same Shewchuk/Si lineage as `triangle`, constrained Delaunay with boundary preservation), but its `pyvista`/`vtk` transitive dependency weight (§2) should be re-examined at that time — possibly via `tetgen`'s lower-level C API bindings without the `pyvista` wrapper, mirroring how `triangle` was added as a thin, dependency-light binding for the 2-D case.

### 4.3 Solver strategy: iterative with a problem-specific preconditioner is not optional

**Decision: an in-house `mt3d_tri` may not attempt a direct sparse solve at any mesh size intended to resolve realistic MT structure.** §3's measurements show unstructured tetrahedral direct solves become intractable at DOF counts smaller than what `mt3d.py` already handles on a structured grid — the "small mesh, direct solve" research-only path that worked for `mt3d.py` (4,096-cell padded mesh, both benchmarks passing) does **not** have a size window here that is simultaneously (a) fast enough to solve directly and (b) fine/large enough to resolve a real receiver-graded structure with adequate skin-depth extent. A preconditioned Krylov solver (multigrid or shifted-Laplacian, same options M6 §4.2 named for the structured case) is a hard prerequisite for any dataset-generation-capable `mt3d_tri`, not a future refinement — and designing/validating one in-house is, per M6's own characterization, "a distinct, non-trivial research task" in its own right.

### 4.4 Scope

**Decision: `mt3d_tri` is not scheduled as a near-term deliverable.** Unlike `mt3d.py` (M6 FAIL-for-production, but a viable small-mesh direct-solve research prototype existed and was built), this gate finds **no analogous safe research-only window** for an unstructured tetrahedral solver without a preconditioner — the direct-solve cost wall is hit at a smaller, more restrictive scale than the structured case, per §3's measured (not extrapolated) 13.4-minute solve at 67,811 DOF, and even the smallest benchmarked mesh (2,175 DOF, 0.158 s) is likely too coarse to usefully resolve a graded near-station region plus multi-skin-depth padding simultaneously, since 3-D unstructured refinement cannot exploit tensor-product coarsening the way a padded structured grid can.

## 5. Gate verdict

**Gate result: FAIL, for both production and an unqualified research-only prototype.** A research-only `mt3d_tri` becomes viable only after a follow-on ADR resolves the preconditioner question (§4.3) — that is a prerequisite gate of its own, not something this benchmark can wave through with a smaller `maximum_cells` ceiling the way `mt3d.py` did. Building `tet_mesh_gen.py` (M18) or `tri_fem3d.py` (M19) now, ahead of that, would be effort spent on a solver that cannot be run at a mesh resolution useful for anything beyond a toy identity check.

## 6. Consequences / recommended path forward

- **M18-M20 (mesh generator, solver, dataset/agent wiring) are not started by this ADR.** They remain correctly ordered *after* a preconditioner feasibility study, which does not yet exist for either the structured (`mt3d.py`) or unstructured case in this codebase.
- If an in-house 3-D solver remains a genuine priority, the next concrete, right-sized piece of work is a **preconditioner feasibility sub-ADR**, scoped narrowly: prototype a shifted-Laplacian or geometric-multigrid preconditioner against the *same* structured curl-curl operator `mt3d.py` already assembles (smaller, better-understood, already-validated physics), measure real iteration counts and wall-clock at the DOF scale M6 found infeasible for direct solve (~216,000-512,000 cells). If that succeeds, the same preconditioning strategy is the one to carry into an unstructured `mt3d_tri` next, rather than developing two independent preconditioners in parallel.
- Absent that, the responsible alternative already exists and needs no new work: **`ModEm3DAdapter` (`modem3d.py`), physics-validated against a real compiled `Mod3DMT` binary since 2026-07-29**, is the production 3-D path per M6/M7, and it is structured/rectilinear-only — the same limitation this ADR's gate leaves unresolved for the unstructured case. For 3-D structure that genuinely requires an unstructured/topography-conforming mesh, `Mare2DEMAdapter`'s 2.5-D triangular approach (M13, real-binary-validated) is the closest currently-verified capability, with the caveat that 2.5-D cannot represent true 3-D (along-strike-varying) structure.
- This ADR does not revisit or weaken M16's `trifem2d.py` (2-D unstructured, nodal, both benchmarks passing) — that milestone's TE/TM scalar decoupling sidesteps every issue raised here, and remains the correct, already-shipped tool for triangular-mesh 2-D work including topography.
